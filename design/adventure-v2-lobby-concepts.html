The Threshold

Today, beginning a session is a status flip behind one button. But the minutes before a story starts are the oldest theatre there is — the house lights, the hush, the match. Six concepts in two parts — three ways to stage the wait, then three things to do together inside it — each played on the same miniature stage: the cast arrives, the dark gathers, the Director begins.

I

Striking the Match

The room assembles itself out of light. Every arrival is one small flame at the table's edge and one step brighter; beginning the session is a struck match — the candle catches, the page warms, and the first line writes itself in wet ink.

the begin
One bright match-strike flash, then the candle pool blooms across the sheet over ~2 seconds, the title illuminates, and the Director’s opening flows in as live ink. Nobody misses the moment it starts.
the cost
Cheapest of the three — it’s a choreography of the light-pass layers we already shipped, gated on session status. No new objects.
II

The Overture

The dark remembers. While the table gathers, lines from past sessions drift up through the unlit page like ghost ink — gilded lines still glowing — so the waiting room quietly replays the story so far. Arriving means signing: your name flourishes across the page in your ink, then settles into the signature line.

III

The Table Sets Itself

Arrival is physical. The stage is a bare table; when the first player arrives the sheet unrolls, and each arriving writer’s inkwell lands on the wood in their colour. The dice settle. The quill lies waiting. Beginning lifts the quill and lights the candle.

part two · the room is not silent

What people do while they wait

The first three concepts stage the wait — but nobody in them ever does anything except arrive. These three put hands in the gathering: talking, betting, writing. Each ends the same way: the candle lights, and whatever the room was doing resolves into the story beginning. They layer onto any skin from part one.

IV

Voices in the Dark

A real pre-game chat — but built as murmurs, not a chat panel. Voices appear in the dark around the unlit page, each in its owner’s ink; the audience murmurs in grey. Nothing is logged, nothing compiles, everything fades. When the Director begins, the room hushes — the voices drift out, and only the page speaks.

the honest risk
“Why can’t we talk during play?” is one question closer. The hush answers it with theatre — curtain up, house quiet — but it is a chat feature returning to a surface we deliberately stripped of one. Ephemerality is the fence: no history means it can never become a second story channel.
V

The Wager

Before the session, the dark places its bets: one-line predictions about tonight, pinned at the room’s rim. They cost nothing and pay nothing — glory only. When the last line is written, the slips settle: the ones that came true are stamped in gold.

the law
Free, always — glory, not gold. A paid wager would be the House buying outcomes through the side door, so this stays permanently ungildable. Retention engine dressed as a parlour game; it also seeds the next session (“unsettled wagers carry over”).
VI

The Warm-up

The Director leaves one question on the unlit page — “one line: what did your character dream last night?” Arriving players answer in their ink. The Director can lift the best answer, and when the candle lights, it becomes the opening line. The wait writes the story.

Arriving means writing — a one-line answer, in character, in your ink, on the page everyone can see. It’s an icebreaker, a voice warm-up, and a re-entry into character after a week away, in a single gesture. Watchers see the answers accumulate: the show has already started, softly.
the begin
The Director taps the line that sings; the candle lights; the opening grows out of the chosen answer. Nothing else on the platform could do this — the lobby literally feeds the manuscript. Unchosen answers fade with the dark; the chosen one is the only survivor.
the cost
Needs one Director input (the question — with a default deck so it’s zero-effort: “what did you dream / what’s in your pocket / what do you fear tonight”), one answer slot per player, one pick. Smallest of the three social concepts, and the most on-brand for a writing platform.
VII

The Temperature

The Director takes the room’s temperature before lighting the candle: one question, a few options, and everyone present — cast in their inks, the dark in grey — leans. Warmth gathers where the room leans. It measures the night; it never decides the story. The Director begins knowing.

Leaning is the lightest possible gesture — one tap, no words to find, no chat to moderate — but every tap is visible connection: your ink lands on the page next to your tablemates’, and the dark’s grey drops gather beside yours. The room literally watches itself agree, split, or surprise the Director.
the begin
Beginning closes the question. The warm option keeps its heat as the candle lights — the Director walks into the night knowing the table wants blood, and the audience knows the Director knows. Nothing prints to the story; the temperature is backstage truth, not canon.
not a vote
Deliberately non-binding, and it must stay that way: a vote decides, a temperature informs. No winner is enacted, so it never collides with crossroads law or “gold never votes.” Mechanically it’s the ballot machinery in its third hood (Director-framed options + free leans from everyone) — cheap to build, and it shares one composer with VI: the Director’s pre-game question, answered in ink or by leaning.

my recommendation

A skin from part one, one question with two answers from part two

The threshold has two layers and they compose freely. The skin: build I (arrival-as-light, the match-strike begin) with II’s ghost ink drifting in the waiting dark — cheapest wow, and the overture doubles as the “previously” recap we already owe. The hands: chat (IV) is out — the owner’s call, and the right one: it’s the only concept here that needs moderation and competes with Discord. Its replacement is better: VI and VII are one feature — the Director leaves a question on the unlit page, answered either in a line of your ink (the warm-up) or by leaning on an option (the temperature). One composer, two modes; cast answers in ink, everyone leans in drops. And V (the wager) belongs in the core build after all — it is the audience’s own game, and its settle step rides the end-of-session modal that already exists: epilogue, cliffhanger, and now which wagers came true.

the audience’s night — the thread, start to finish Arriving is light: every watcher who shows up early is a mote at the rim and a rising count the cast can feel — presence is participation before a single tap. The temperature: the dark’s grey drops land beside the cast’s ink; the audience helps set the tone of the night before it starts. The wager: theirs alone — the cast plays the night, the dark bets on it, and stays to the last line to see the slips settle in gold. The hush: when the candle lights, the room changing state is something that happens to them too — then their in-play hands take over (lean, gild, the Stranger’s ballots). And if the story keeps a chair for the dark, the threshold gains a flourish later: a warm-up question addressed to the Stranger, answered by the house in moon-silver.
under the skin — identical for every combination Cast presence rides the existing 5-second session poll (one lastSeen upsert). The watch page gets a real pre-session state instead of an empty sheet. Followers can be told “the table is gathering” before the begin notification, and the Browse live row can show gathering tables as “about to begin.” The Director’s begin control becomes a readiness line: “Lyra and Vael are at the table · 3 watching from the dark.” Wagers, warm-up answers, and leans are session-scoped rows that never touch turns or compile; the warm-up answer the Director lifts becomes plain opening text; wagers settle inside the existing end-of-session modal. Spectator presence must run on draft sessions (today it only heartbeats while active) — that one change powers the motes, the counts, and the door.
