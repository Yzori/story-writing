The Hollow Lantern · Act II
Scene Four — The Customs House After Dark
Wren
Director

The customs house has been locked since the drowning, but locks in Port Merrow answer to salt before they answer to keys. Inside, the ledgers are stacked like headstones, and one drawer stands open — recently, judging by the dust. On the desk, a letter sealed in grey wax. The harbor bell rings once. No one should be at the rope.

Ilsa — you came here for the ledgers. The bell says you're not alone.

Ilsa did not flinch at the bell. Flinching was for people who still believed the harbor could surprise them. She set her bag on the drowned man's desk, unrolled her instruments as if this were any other appointment, and only then let herself look at the letter. Grey wax. The customs office sealed in black, the church in red. Nobody in Port Merrow sealed in grey — nobody living, at least.

"You can come out," she said to the dark between the shelves. "I charge for examinations, but eavesdropping is free."

The dark between the shelves sighed, and Pin stepped out of it, hands up, grin first. Getting caught was an art, and the trick was to look like you'd planned it. "In my defense," Pin said, "I was here before you. That makes you the intruder, and me the witness."

Overhead, the bell rings a second time — then the rope goes slack. Whoever was ringing has let go. Footsteps on the harbor boards, unhurried, coming this way. And the grey seal on the letter, for those close enough to see it, is carrying a watermark: the sigil of the Lighthouse Office, retired these forty years.

Every story needs a table.

An adventure is written by a small cast — one Director who runs the world, two to four writers who each play a character. Bring friends, or take an open seat from the board. Tables are matched on pace first: a table that writes at your speed is a table that finishes.
