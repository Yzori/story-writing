Quiloria — Color Concepts

Three full directions, each shown as the same app screen in dark and light. The brief: keep the gold-on-black the homepage already nails, and make light mode Apple-slick — porcelain and ink, not beige. Every text value shown passes WCAG AA on its background (ratios annotated).

CONCEPT 01

Gilded Obsidian

Recommended

The homepage black, promoted to the whole app. Surfaces are true near-black — a whisper of warmth (you feel it, you can't name it), never brown. Gold is jewelry: CTAs, the logo, active states, little else. Light mode is porcelain — Apple's white-on-soft-gray layering with hairline borders and feather shadows, warmed by maybe 2% so it doesn't go clinical. Accent inks stay saturated and jewel-like.

CONCEPT 02

The Gallery

Full Apple. Pure neutral greys — no warmth in any surface — and gold as the only color in the chrome. Dark is true black with graphite layers (#000 → #1C1C1E territory); light is white on #F5F5F7. Primary buttons in light mode are black, not gold — gold is reserved for brand moments, highlights and earnings. Maximum slick, minimum atmosphere.

CONCEPT 03

Bookbinder's Green

The wildcard. Clothbound-classic: deep green-black velvet with gold leaf — the palette of old Penguin hardbacks, reading lamps, and private libraries. Dark surfaces carry a forest tint instead of brown; gold pops even harder against green-black than against neutral black. Light mode is ivory-white with green ink as a second voice. A colored dark, not a brown gloom — and nobody else's app looks like this.

CONCEPT 04

Midnight Scriptorium

Claude's pick

The unconstrained answer. Quiloria's whole vocabulary is ink — Ink Drops, the Ink Dock, the quill — and real writing ink is iron-gall blue-black, not brown. This is the illuminated manuscript palette: lapis and gold leaf on vellum. Dark mode is a true midnight — deep ink-blue-black that gets slightly more chromatic as surfaces rise — with warm vellum text and candle gold. Gold sits opposite blue on the wheel, so it glows harder here than on any neutral or brown black. Light mode is the page itself: warm white vellum, iron-gall ink text, gilt details. Warm light, cool shadow — the physics of reading by lamplight.

Why I believe in it (and why it's contentious): this project has a hard rule — "no blue, ever" — written after a blue-violet dark mode failed. I think that postmortem blamed the hue when the crime was execution: those surfaces were desaturated gray-violet with ashen pastel accents — that's what reads as generic SaaS, not blueness itself. No SaaS dashboard looks like lapis-and-gold-leaf. Three real arguments: (1) gold is blue's complement — it burns brighter on midnight than on any black or brown; (2) cool neutral chrome flatters colorful cover art the way warm umber never can — galleries hang art on neutral walls for a reason; (3) the warmth belongs in the light (text, gold, lamp glows), not the shadows — warm-light-on-cool-dark is literally how lamplight works at night, and it's why the old all-warm dark mode kept collapsing into brown mush. The honest counterargument: you have consistently chosen warmth every time, and taste legitimately outranks theory.

Where I'd land

Two different answers to two different questions. Safest great outcome: Concept 01 (Gilded Obsidian) — it matches your stated taste, unifies homepage and app, and can't miss. Steal the Gallery's black button for light-mode secondary actions. What I'd actually ship if it were my product: Concept 04 (Midnight Scriptorium). Ink is the brand; midnight-and-gold is the rarest, most "magical library" pairing on this page, and it makes the gold — the thing you love most — glow hardest. Judge it at night, with the lights low, next to 01. If 04 still reads "corporate" to your eye after that, ship 01 and never look back.
